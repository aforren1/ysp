Flash and click: what a browser says about audio-visual timing

Browser timing is not rig timing. Both times this page compares are the browser's estimates. The flash's time is when the browser began the frame, before its compositor and the display; the click's time comes from the browser's own figure for its audio output, which no one checked on this device. Neither is when light or sound reached you. A photodiode and a microphone on an oscilloscope measure that; a browser never does. How the web backend works.

What it does

Each trial plans a flash (a light disk for 3 frames) and a short click for the same time, 0.3 s ahead. Then it compares the two records: the frame's onset as ysp/screen.h estimates it, and the click's onset as ysp/audio.h estimates it from the browser's output timestamps. It also records every audio callback, to show how regular they were.

Warning: about one flash a second for 20 seconds. Set the volume low; the click is short and quiet. On a phone, turn off silent mode.

This page uses the - audio path. A cross-origin isolated page gets the AudioWorklet build with threads; every other page gets the ScriptProcessorNode build.
