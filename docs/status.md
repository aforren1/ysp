# Status of each header

This page is reference. It lists, for each header, what is built, what
was checked and measured, and what is not built or not measured. It also
lists the files to copy for each header and the hardware that ran each
header. The [README](../README.md) has the short table.

A header needs the OS, the C standard library and the ysp headers in its
row of [Files to copy](#files-to-copy); a few also need an outside
library (SDL3, miniaudio, liblsl).

## Verified on hardware

Three machines have run these headers on real devices: a Windows 11
laptop (i7-1360P, Intel Iris Xe driving its internal 1920x1200 60 Hz
panel, Realtek speakers), from 2026-10-09 a Linux desktop (Debian 12,
liquorix 6.11 kernel with the PDS scheduler, i5-14500, AMD RX 6600 on
1920x1080 60 Hz monitors as two X screens, Mesa 22.3.6, PulseAudio 16
on Intel HDA; the screen, rt, audio, serial and device rows say what ran there), and from
2026-11-04 an Apple M2 Mac mini (below). No photodiode, photometer, loopback cable,
external monitor, parallel port, response box, trigger box or
LabStreamer was attached (docs/screen.md, docs/audio.md,
docs/device.md). The one serial device was a Teensy 4.0 echo board on
native USB (the serial and device rows), on this laptop, on the Linux
desktop and, on 2026-11-05, on the Mac below. "Sim" below means simulated displays,
fake transports, virtual serial pairs, null audio devices or software
renderers, in tests and CI.

An Apple M2 Mac mini (macOS 27.0.1, Apple clang 21, arm64, APFS) ran
the tests of every header on 2026-11-04 with the `macos` and
`macos-sanitizers` presets (73 of 73 in each; 74 with ysp/serial.h's
new test), without SDL3, miniaudio or
liblsl: ysp/screen.h, ysp/gfx.h, ysp/video.h and ysp/audio.h ran only
their simulated and null paths, ysp/net.h only its fake liblsl. The serial
and device loopbacks ran on a pseudo-terminal pair. No device was
attached. Later runs on the same Mac (2026-11-04 and 2026-11-05) used
SDL3 3.4.0, miniaudio 0.11.25 and ANGLE from Electron 43.8.3: the screen,
audio and video rows give them. The macOS timings are ysp/rt.h's clock,
waits and ring, the sync costs of ysp/dataset.h and ysp/trials.h,
ysp/video.h's AVFoundation decode, ysp/audio.h on the built-in
speakers and HDMI, and ysp/screen.h's display probe on the M27Q, all
on 2026-11-05 with a Screen Sharing session open (the display probe
also ran again without it), and ysp/serial.h and ysp/device.h on the
Teensy 4.0 echo board (2026-11-05, other work stopped); every other macOS
result in this page is functional.

every file in the row into one `ysp/` folder on your include path. Where a
row has more than one header, define each implementation macro once
([Quick start](../README.md#quick-start), step 2); a header compiles the implementations it includes
unless your file already did.

## Headers

Each entry gives the prefix, the platforms, what the header needs, its
purpose, and its status by version, newest first.

### ysp/rt.h

- Header: [ysp/rt.h](../include/ysp/rt.h)
- Prefix: `yrt_`, `YRT_`
- Platforms: Windows, Linux, macOS, WebAssembly (Emscripten, no scheduling ladder)
- Needs: OS only; Tracy, opt-in

Purpose: The shared base: monotonic clock, deadline waits, thread elevation, a one-shot deadline worker, a background pump for inference between trials, a wait-free event ring for the rig's log, clock correlation, a device clock fit (a box's or a board's own clock mapped to the rig's: lower envelope, bracketed reads or least squares, with unwrap and restart), per-user cache and config folders with ownership checks, and instrumentation macros (nothing, Tracy, or the ring). The transport headers and the async layers of the adaptive headers use it.

Status:

- Bindings: `ysp-rt` 0.10.2 (Python) and the MEX `ysp_rt` 1.0.0: the clock (the one `ysp.serial.now()` and `ysp.parallel.now()` read, checked), deadline waits (the GIL released), clock correlation, the device clock fit, the virtual clock and the per-user folders, in seconds; worker, pump, ring and the scheduling ladder are not bound. On Windows a binding's wait returns the header's lateness (median 0.1 us with the default spin); the host's return adds 8 us in Python, 37 to 40 us in MATLAB and 65 us in Octave at the median. Tests pass on Windows and WSL2 (Python), MATLAB R2023a and Octave 10.1. [Bindings](rt.md#bindings).
- v0.10.2 (Linux; measured as v0.8.1 before the merge with v0.9.1, numbered v0.9.2 before the merge with v0.10.1): `yrt_thread_elevate()` takes SCHED_FIFO under an `rtprio` grant when FIFO 80 is refused (the grant above 50, else FIFO 1). On POSIX the worker and pump threads start SCHED_OTHER instead of inheriting their creator's policy. [Linux](rt.md#linux-real-time-priority-v0102). After the merge with v0.10.1 (2026-11-05): the Linux tests pass, `dev-thr-00` is killed, and rt.toml gives the expected result on Linux except its Windows-only `dir-` mutants (6 survive, `dir-10` is killed); the kqueue code was not compiled on Linux.
- v0.10.1 (2026-11-05): on macOS a thread without the TIME_CONSTRAINT rung waits on a one-shot kevent timer flagged `NOTE_CRITICAL` (absolute, mach time), in a kqueue the thread owns (opened by its first wait, closed by `yrt_thread_cleanup()` or its exit, not inherited by a fork child); a worker without the rung waits on its own kqueue, woken by submit, cancel and close. Darwin coalesced the old `nanosleep()` and timed condition waits by a quarter to a half of the wait (a 10 ms wait 2 to 5 ms late), far beyond the 200 us spin; QoS class, latency tier 0 and `NOTE_LEEWAY` 0 did not remove it. Measured on the M2 Mac mini (quiet machine): waits of 1 to 10 ms without a spin 30 to 41 us late at the median on an ordinary thread and on the main thread of an SDL window (at 5 ms: 38 and 36 us, against 1268 us before on both); with the default spin 0.1 to 0.2 us on every thread (1018 us before); TIME_CONSTRAINT threads keep `nanosleep()` (14 to 16 us against the timer's 42 to 45 us). `yser_pulse()` widths -31 to -7 us median (+0.7 to +2.2 ms before). The printf format attributes are spelled `__format__(__printf__, ...)`, so a MEX build's `#define printf mexPrintf` no longer drops the check (0 warnings, 2 before). New `tests/adapt/rt_test_darwin_wait.h`; ASan, UBSan and ThreadSanitizer clean; 7 of 7 new mutants killed (`mac-05` to `mac-11`). [The wait on macOS](rt.md#the-wait-on-macos-v0101).
- v0.10.0 (2026-11-04): on macOS `yrt_now_ns()` reads `mach_absolute_time()` and `yrt_ticks_to_ns()` converts mach units (CoreAudio host times, `CVTimeStamp`); `CLOCK_MONOTONIC` counted in whole microseconds there (correlation width 1000 ns, now 83 ns) and takes frequency adjustments. Other platforms unchanged. First run on a Mac (M2 Mac mini, macOS 27.0.1, Apple clang 21): `rt_test` passes, Release and ASan with UBSan; an unprivileged thread got `TIME_CONSTRAINT` (read back as asked) and `FIFO` when Mach refused a 100 ms computation; the pump got `BELOW_NORMAL`; the per-user folders are under `~/Library`. 4 of 4 new mutants killed (macOS only). Measured 2026-11-05 (AC power, no other load): `yrt_now_ns()` 12.3 ns; 2 ms waits at `TIME_CONSTRAINT` 10 to 16 us late at the median without a spin, 0.0 us with the default spin; a ring push 13 ns. [macOS](rt.md#macos-v0100).
- v0.9.1 (2026-10-27): `yrt_sleep_ns()` near `INT64_MAX` ("forever") on the virtual clock saturates its deadline and moves the clock to its end; the plain sum overflowed and the wait returned at once. The real clock's sum is unsigned and was safe, and so was `yrt_pump_wait()`. MSVC, MinGW-w64, WSL2 gcc with ASan and UBSan; 2 of 2 new mutants killed. [A wait without end](rt.md#a-wait-without-end-v091).
- v0.9.0: a virtual clock for simulated runs (`yrt_virtual_begin()`, virtual timers), and `yrt_real_ns()`, the real clock for costs on either clock: the driver thread's waits move it, other threads wait for it, and ysp/screen.h's SIM backend, ysp/audio.h's null device and ysp/video.h follow it. The CI list of 49 `--sim` and `--null` runs gave the same data files and output in three idle runs and two beside 24 CPU-bound processes (MSVC), and the same data with MinGW-w64; the lines that differ print real CPU time. Real-clock cost inside the run-to-run spread: `yrt_now_ns()` 24.3 to 26.3 ns against 23.5 to 26.1 ns pinned, the screen's per-frame header cost unchanged. 13 of 13 new mutants caught ([Virtual clock](rt.md#virtual-clock-v090)).
- v0.8.0 (the [Conventions](conventions.md): `yrt_now_ns()` and every time `int64_t` ns, `YRT_S()`/`YRT_MS()`/`YRT_US()`, worker and pump open/close, `yrt_fit_open()`; 2 mutants added, both caught).
- v0.7.0 (per-user folders: `yrt_user_dir()`, the rules of gfx's program cache folder, tested on Windows, WSL2 and emcc).
- v0.6.0 (the device clock fit: on synthetic devices within tens of us of the truth for dense pairs, ms for sparse pairs behind a 16 ms timer; 17 of 17 mutants caught; no real device yet).
- v0.5.0: event ring stress-tested with 2 to 8 producers (no torn or lost record unaccounted for), ThreadSanitizer clean; a push costs 11 to 37 ns natively and a TIME_CRITICAL producer's p99.9 stays near 2 us while 7 others push, against 0.1 to 0.5 ms behind a lock; Tracy mode built against Tracy v0.14.1 ([design notes and tables](rt.md)). Pump built and run on Windows, Linux and under node (Emscripten); on MinGW the worker and pump threads inherit the x87 control word so a thread computes the same bits as the caller, ThreadSanitizer clean including a wait-versus-stop hammer; the deadline worker as before: Windows measured with [rt_jitter](../examples/rt/jitter.c); Linux built and run under WSL2, worker clean under ThreadSanitizer; CI compiles the macOS backend, which had not run on a Mac (v0.10.0 ran it). No test host grants CAP_SYS_NICE, so the SCHED_FIFO and Mach time-constraint rungs are unexercised (on macOS both need no privilege; see v0.10.0).

### ysp/audio.h

- Header: [ysp/audio.h](../include/ysp/audio.h)
- Prefix: `yau_`, `YAU_`
- Platforms: Windows (WASAPI shared; exclusive runs but this laptop's driver buffers about 100 ms); macOS (CoreAudio with the HAL's time stamps, hog mode; one Mac, the built-in speakers and HDMI timed); Linux (ALSA exclusive on a card's hw: device, AUTO there; PulseAudio with its stream timing info; output and capture, duplex on one card; tier 3); Web Audio in Chrome and Firefox (AudioWorklet and ScriptProcessorNode, the browser's estimates only)
- Needs: ysp/rt.h, miniaudio 0.11.25

Purpose: Sound at a time, and its capture, on the ysp/rt.h clock: `yau_play_at(buf, t)` plans the first sample on the device frame t falls on, through a fit of the device clock against the ysp_rt clock, and records where it landed (stream frame, its time from that fit, residual, tier) in the handle and in the event ring. ### ysp/video.h

- Header: [ysp/video.h](../include/ysp/video.h)
- Prefix: `yvid_`, `YVID_`
- Platforms: Windows and Linux X11 (GLX_OML) through ysp/gfx.h; MP4 through Media Foundation on Windows, AVFoundation on macOS (v0.7.0) and FFmpeg on Linux (opt-in, v0.8.0); any platform on the simulated display and CPU decode
- Needs: ysp/gfx.h, ysp/timeline.h; pl_mpeg (optional); FFmpeg's headers and, at run time, libraries (optional, Linux)

Purpose: A movie as a stimulus: on each display frame the frame due at the predicted onset by ysp/timeline.h's lead rule, never by counting frames; a cadence that judders (3:2) is shown and named, slips against the display grid are found and named, and every display frame gets a record (frame, due time, flip onset, decision and reason).
